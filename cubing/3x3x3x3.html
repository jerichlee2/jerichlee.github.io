Solving the 3×3×3×3

My first solve of the four-dimensional 3×3×3×3 puzzle in Hyperspeedcube.

The saved log records a 45-turn scramble and a move count of 1,017 STM.

Download my solution (.hsc). Open the file in Hyperspeedcube 1.x to view the solve.

A cube, one dimension higher

A square has four line-segment sides. A cube has six square faces. Continue that pattern into four dimensions and the boundary is made of eight cubes. This four-dimensional hypercube is called a tesseract: it has 16 vertices, 32 edges, 24 square faces, and eight cubic cells.

The extra dimension here is another spatial direction, not time. A point needs four coordinates instead of three. The view on the screen is a projection of that higher-dimensional geometry, much as a drawing can represent a solid cube on a flat sheet of paper.

Even the stickers become cubes

On an ordinary 3×3, each face has nine square stickers. On the 3×3×3×3, each cubic cell has 27 cubic “stickers,” for 216 in total. Its 80 colored pieces consist of eight fixed centers, 24 two-colored pieces, 32 three-colored pieces, and 16 four-colored corners. A corner really does need four colors instead of three. Roice Nelson’s 4D solution guide breaks down these piece types.

A 121-digit possibility space

The ordinary 3×3 has about 43 quintillion possible positions. Its 4D counterpart has approximately 1.76 × 10120, a number with 121 digits. That does not mean checking every position to solve it. Familiar ideas from 3D cubing still apply: put pieces in the right places and orientations while preserving what is already solved.
