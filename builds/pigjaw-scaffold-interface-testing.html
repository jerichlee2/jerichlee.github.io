Pigjaw Scaffold–Bone Interface Mechanical Testing

This project explores fixtures for push-out testing at the interface between a biodegradable scaffold and porcine jawbone. During the original 2025 project, we set aside a fixture concept because of concerns that a printed PLA load path might break. In September 2026, I revisited the unused concept through hand calculations and finite element analysis of the supplied reference CAD.

Revision B brings those calculations together with the PLA results from ANSYS Mechanical. Under an assumed 250 N annular load, the final model predicts 0.13137 mm of maximum downward annular movement, about 2.63 times the chosen 0.050 mm target. The clearest finding is a fixture-compliance concern under the modeled conditions.

The analysis is retrospective: it did not drive the 2025 decision. It does not establish observed breakage, a safe operating load, or experimental validation of the fixture.

Geometry and hand calculations

The supplied solid has a 120 × 140 × 30 mm envelope, but only a 15 mm central web above the underside relief. A 25 mm opening is surrounded by a slotted annulus with two narrow connections, each represented by a minimum ligament width of about 6.08 mm. Using the overall 30 mm height as the bending thickness would overestimate the rectangular-section bending stiffness by a factor of eight.

The first calculation replaces the deck with a simply supported 60 mm bridge of reduced width, 75.7 mm. With an assumed PLA modulus of 2.0 GPa, bending plus transverse shear gives 31.77 µm of global deflection at 250 N. Separate local checks examine contact pressure, ligament shear, and sensitivity to the effective bending arm. The bridge does not resolve local annulus and ligament deformation, so its result alone cannot qualify the slotted fixture.

The 250 N force is a literature-context benchmark, not a measured porcine test load. The 50 µm motion budget and PLA screening properties are analysis assumptions. Rev B also treats 500 N and 1000 N as sensitivity cases.

A separate fit issue remains: a nominal 25 mm implant in a 25 mm opening has zero radial clearance. A provisional 0.7 mm radial-clearance starting point would imply a 26.4 mm opening before tolerances, but it is not a validated requirement for this porcine construct. Any enlargement also reduces the annulus width and needs a new structural check.

Retrospective PLA model

The follow-up uses ANSYS Mechanical 2025 R2 and a single isotropic, linear-elastic PLA fixture body with E = 2000 MPa and ν = 0.35. A downward pressure of 0.4901 MPa on the 510.1083 mm² top annulus supplies approximately 250 N total. Bone, scaffold, and interface failure are not modeled.

The two lowest bottom lands have compression-only plate support. Four 16 mm outer-diameter washer patches restrain vertical motion while allowing in-plane movement; two additional locators remove in-plane rigid motion. These idealized hold-downs include no bolt preload, washer bending, or bolt stretch. Contact uses a manual normal-stiffness factor of 10, and large deflection is off.

Rev B records the setup and supplied result screenshots; it is not an independent solver rerun. The final solver project and complete logs were not included in that record.

The final annular Y-displacements range from −0.13137 to −0.054935 mm. Both are downward; the most negative value defines the maximum downward movement. It exceeds the target by 81.37 µm and is about 4.14 times the same-force bridge estimate. The two models have different geometry, restraints, and response measures, so that ratio is not a calibrated correction factor.

Annular fixture motion is not automatically specimen displacement, interface strain, or crosshead measurement error. Relating it to measurement accuracy requires the actual specimen contact and displacement-measurement arrangement.

Mesh, stress, and equilibrium checks

The final M2 mesh contains 941,817 quadratic tetrahedral elements. Its 0.5 mm sizing is local to the four rounded slot-end walls, not the entire fixture. Refining M1 to M2 changes maximum downward annular motion from 0.13134 to 0.13137 mm: about 0.023%, or 0.03 µm.

Stress convergence is less complete. The final maximum principal tensile peaks are 5.5769 MPa averaged and 6.0484 MPa unaveraged. Their M1-to-M2 changes are +3.21% and −5.72%, respectively; the latter misses the chosen 5% successive-change criterion. Both final values are below the assumed 10 MPa normal allowance, but print anisotropy, interlayer weakness, creep, and cracking are absent, and the low-quality-element location relative to the stress peak remains unresolved. This is not a complete strength pass.

The signed vertical reactions provide another consistency check: +355.55 N at the plate and −105.50 N across all four washer patches give +250.05 N against the nominal −250 N load. The 0.05 N residual is 0.020% of the load, using rounded readouts. It is a vertical balance check, not a full moment-balance or solver audit.

What the revision establishes

The unchanged PLA concept does not meet the selected 250 N / 50 µm screen. That identifies a modeled compliance concern; it does not demonstrate fracture or prove that machined metal is mandatory. The same-geometry hand comparisons give aluminum about 34 times PLA’s flexural stiffness and steel about 100 times, but no aluminum or steel FEA or physical qualification is included.

Before experimental use, the next steps are to establish the actual force and accuracy requirements, verify specimen clearance and contact, characterize the print and mounting conditions, resolve local stress uncertainty, and measure the complete assembly’s motion under a representative dummy-specimen load within separately established limits.
